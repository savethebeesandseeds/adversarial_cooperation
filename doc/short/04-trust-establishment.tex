\chapter{Trust Establishment}
\label{short:trust-establishment}

A participant should be able to tend its own source of uncertainty and retain
its private advantages. Yet a secret made alone says nothing about who waits
at the other end of a conversation.

Ask what connects this message, this key, and this session to the intended
party. A challenge rests on some prior binding; more challenges cannot
create that binding from nothing. Make the starting trust visible.

Keep the protocol's secret material to what the shared purpose needs. A secret may be a capability
and a liability at once. The companion separates these questions; it does
not establish moral trust or supply a new identity protocol.
