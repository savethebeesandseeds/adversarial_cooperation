\chapter{Conflict Arbitrage}
\label{short:conflict-arbitrage}

Between positions that appear opposed, there may be ground that neither party
has occupied. Cooperation may begin by finding room to grow there, before
asking either party to surrender what it already holds.

The intuition is to discover value left unused by the conflict, and to ask
whether an intervention can release it while restoring balance. Who gains
from this intervention? Who pays for it? Who is permitted to make it?

The space between two positions is an invitation to investigate. It is not
yet an answer about who should stand there.

\shortlimit{Cryptography does not decide who should intervene or who should bear the risks.}
