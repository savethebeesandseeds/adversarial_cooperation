\chapter{Law Without Referencing the Law}
\label{chap:law-without-referencing}

\researchstatus{Design intuition and open problem}{No judicial protocol,
privacy definition, authority model, or due-process mechanism is established.}

This game is set where an advantage is lost by referencing the rules of the
game. The rules are visible to all parties; what changes is the verification
system.

The proposed aim is to prove that a hidden event follows all the rules without
disclosing the event. A second, more delicate aim is to prove that an event
breaks one or more rules without revealing the event or which rules were
broken.

When freedom is serious, referencing a rule may expose it to modification or
retaliation. The original artifact therefore imagines a jury that can issue a
decision derived from a committed rule set without identifying the decisive
chapter and article: informally, ``\(x\) because of
\(\operatorname{hash}(y)\)'' rather than ``\(x\) because of \(y\).''

This remains only an intuition. A defensible construction must address policy
versioning, authority over the encoded rules, reasons, audit, appeal,
discriminatory hidden inputs, and the risk that secrecy protects domination
rather than due process.
