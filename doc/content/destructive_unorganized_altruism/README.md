# Destructive Unorganized Altruism

Chapter notes — **Author's intuition**.

Watching seagulls fight over food offered to them makes feeding the whole
flock a challenge. The intention is to help every bird, yet access to that
help becomes a contest.

**Author's intuition:** unorganized altruism may provoke or intensify violence
among those it is meant to benefit. Wanting to feed everyone does not, by
itself, organize access to the food.

> Generosity has a distribution problem.

## Questions

- How can help reach everyone when access to that help is itself contested?
- Does generosity require attention to the competition it creates?
- Does feeding create conflict, intensify existing conflict, or make it visible?
- Is the difficulty a shortage of food, unequal access, or both?
- Do the same birds repeatedly obtain food while others go without, or does
  fighting persist even when there appears to be enough for everyone?

## Limits of the observation

Concentrating food in one place or distributing it in unpredictable bursts
are possible explanations to investigate, not details established by the
author's account. The observation does not establish a general causal claim
about seagulls or altruism. These notes leave the solution open.

## What Cryptography Does Not Solve

No cryptographic role has been identified. How to organize access to help
and reduce physical conflict remains an open question.
