\chapter{Addressing Goodhart's Law}
\label{chap:addressing-goodharts-law}

\researchstatus{Open problem; citations needed}{This chapter does not claim a
cryptographic solution. Campbell's Law, the Cobra Effect, and reward hacking
remain items in the bibliography queue.}

\begin{quote}
When a measure becomes a target, it can cease to be a good measure.
\end{quote}

The original note is candid: we do not yet know how to solve this problem, but
the book must confront it.

The current intuitions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Optimizing harmony may stagnate the system's dynamics.
  \item An objective might need to evolve, perhaps through controlled
  variation rather than a permanent fixed target.
  \item A system can assume that its objective is flawed and reward discovery
  of those flaws.
  \item Hard problems require humble solutions. An immortal and inflexible
  system would betray the natural world; succession, change, appeal, and
  expiration must remain possible.
\end{itemize}

Cryptography may help commit to versions, randomize audits, or prove that a
declared process was followed. It cannot decide which objective is good.
