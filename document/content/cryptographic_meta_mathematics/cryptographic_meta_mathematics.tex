\chapter{Cryptographic Meta-Mathematics}
\label{chap:cryptographic-meta-mathematics}

\researchstatus{Analogy and open problem}{The connection to Goedel's
incompleteness theorems is not established. ``Hidden axiom'' is presently a
metaphor for a private policy, preference, constraint, or witness.}

The motivating idea is important to the project: cooperating adversaries may
retain individual advantages while proving that a shared condition holds.

The original note compared extensions of an axiomatic system, such as the
continuum hypothesis or large-cardinal axioms, with a game containing public
and private rules. It imagined that:

\begin{itemize}
  \item some axioms or constraints are public;
  \item some remain private and constitute a party's strategic advantage; and
  \item a proof of compliant behavior must account for the relevant public and
  private conditions.
\end{itemize}

A technically narrower future question is whether a party can commit to a
private policy or constraint set and prove in zero knowledge that a proposed
action satisfies a public relation involving that committed object. No such
relation or proof system is supplied by this chapter.
