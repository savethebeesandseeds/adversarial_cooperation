\chapter{Cryptographic Meta-Mathematics}
\label{short:cryptographic-meta-mathematics}

Cooperating adversaries may wish to retain individual advantages while
showing that a shared condition holds. The original note imagines public
axioms alongside hidden ones: a common game in which each party keeps
something of its own.

For a possible construction, begin with a smaller object: a private policy
or constraint to which a party could commit. Ask what a proposed action must
satisfy, and whether that condition could be proved without exposing the
private object.

The hidden-axiom comparison remains an analogy. Its connection to
incompleteness is unestablished, and this chapter supplies neither the precise
relation nor the private proof.
