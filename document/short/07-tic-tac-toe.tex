\chapter{Tic-Tac-Toe without Revealing the Strategy}
\label{short:tic-tac-toe}

Can I show that I know how to avoid defeat while keeping the strategy to
myself? The question protects a kind of autonomy: cooperation need not demand
every private advantage.

First say what the strategy must accomplish. In the companion's fixed game,
a disclosed policy can be checked against every legal opposing continuation
from the empty board. The code can also check a disclosed opening of a
commitment to that policy.

Keeping the policy hidden during proof remains unfinished. Even a future
proof would concern the stated game and condition. It would not promise that
the player follows the policy tomorrow.

\shortlimit{A proof cannot decide whether avoiding defeat is the right objective.}
