\chapter{Aggregation Disadvantage}
\label{short:aggregation-disadvantage}

Two beautiful things placed together do not necessarily make a third thing
with twice the beauty.

When allies join, their strengths may fail to add. Attention may be divided;
custody may become disputed; one contribution may obscure another. A merger
can change the very thing it was meant to preserve.

Ask what must be shared, and what should remain distinct. There may be a form
of cooperation in which each helps the other continue separately.

The question is not answered by counting how many have joined. We must first
understand what their joining does to what they value.

\shortlimit{Cryptography does not decide which things should be joined.}
